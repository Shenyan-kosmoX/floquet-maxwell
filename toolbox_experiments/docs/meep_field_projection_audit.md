# Meep field-coupling audit

The archived LPOR `r32_pad1` and `r48_pad1/ringdown_volume.npz` files contain complex E, D, and H components on a common interpolated grid, a scalar dielectric output, coordinates, and cubature weights. These support field-shape diagnostics, but do **not** yet provide a validated electromagnetic coupling integral. The reported hard-cylinder-mask estimate S=0.064526 at resolution 32 exceeds 1/16, the bound for an exactly represented positive-energy mode with epsilon=16 inside that same mask; resolution 48 gives 0.062029. This is evidence of an inconsistent discretized mask/metric combination, not enhanced physical coupling. The hard-mask result should not be used as the modal backend's calibrated coefficient.

Meep's official subpixel-averaging and field-array documentation explains the relevant distinction: discontinuous scalar media can produce effective anisotropic dielectric tensors on the staggered Yee lattice, while exported E/D/material arrays are interpolated to common voxel centers. Multiplying an interpolated scalar epsilon by interpolated E is generally not the same operation as Meep's internal constitutive update; interpolating a field product also differs from multiplying interpolated fields. The derivative of the subpixel material operator with respect to the cylinder's physical epsilon is not simply a hard indicator or `(epsilon_output-1)/15`. Consequently, a correct discrete perturbation overlap requires the appropriate material-operator derivative and field metric, or an independently converged continuum representation; the common-grid scalar output alone does not establish either.

An independent read of the stored arrays reproduces the fractional-mask estimates S=0.0561324805 and 0.0566493600. Replacing the scalar-energy denominator by the common-grid real integral of E*·D gives 0.0502578397 and 0.0525654864; the corresponding E*·D to epsilon|E|² denominator ratios are 1.11689004 and 1.07769116. The discrepancies shrink with resolution but remain material at the current grids. E*·D is a useful constitutive diagnostic, yet this substitution alone does not repair the numerator's missing discrete material derivative, nor does it establish outgoing-QNM normalization. No field-derived normalization or coupling should be labeled fully verified from these numbers.

The current single-mode bridge instead uses the independently rerun, same-grid static dielectric derivative, `S_calibrated=-2[d Re(omega)/d epsilon]/Re(omega)`: the measured values are 0.057764554 for perturbation h=0.1 and 0.057763704 for h=0.05, differing by approximately 1.47e-5 relatively. This is a reproducible **Meep-calibrated effective single-mode temporal approximation**, with a well-tested first-order static response. It is not a full QNM projection, and static first-order agreement does not certify its quadratic dynamic coefficient, sideband-dependent radiation, or fixed-loss closure. Retain the direct derivative and all unadjusted field diagnostics separately; use the calibrated value for this explicitly labeled bridge, without rescaling the fields to manufacture agreement.
